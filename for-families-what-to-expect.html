For families: what the next year looks like

You have been living inside this for years. Here is what changes when he walks through our door, and what does not.

Joseph’s Home · Saucier, Mississippi · 2 min read · updated August 2026

“Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2 · ESV

You are probably exhausted in a way that people who have not lived it do not understand. Before anything else: what happens next is not on your shoulders alone any more.

The first month

He will be adjusting to a schedule, chores, church, and men who will not be manipulated. Expect one difficult phone call around the third week — the one where he is miserable and persuasive and wants to come home. It happens so reliably that we warn men about it in advance.

Your line for that call: I love you. I am not coming to get you. Talk to the staff tonight. Then hang up and go be with somebody.

Months two to six

Work starts. A rhythm appears. This is often the season families feel most hopeful and most afraid at the same time, because you have seen good stretches before and know how they ended.

Watch for boring, unspectacular things: he calls on a normal day. He asks about you. He keeps a job. He mentions the name of a man from the house. Those are worth more than any dramatic declaration.

Months six to twelve, and after

He begins to be useful to newer men. He may want to talk about amends, or about what he did. Let him — and do not feel obliged to have forgiven everything on his schedule. Both things can be true: you can be proud of him and still be angry about what happened.

The residential year ends but the plan does not. Two more years of mentorship follow. If he starts skipping it, that is worth a conversation.

What we ask of you

  • Do not send money without telling staff. It is the most common way families accidentally cause harm.
  • Do not negotiate the rules on his behalf. If he has a complaint, it belongs to him and to staff.
  • Do not compare him to anybody — not a cousin, not who he was at twenty.
  • Tell us what you are seeing. You know him better than we do.
  • Get support of your own. Celebrate Recovery has meetings for families; Al-Anon and Nar-Anon are on the coast. Do not carry this alone.

Our Hope family ministry takes a whole-family approach, with weekend activities that care for the families of the men here. Ask us about it — you are not an outsider to this work.
