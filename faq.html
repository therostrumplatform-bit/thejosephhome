About the home

What is Joseph’s Home?

A faith-based sober living home for men in Saucier, Mississippi. Men who have completed a rehab program live here, work, attend church, and are discipled. We offer a three-year discipleship plan: twelve months living in the home, then two years of self-directed mentorship.

Are you a rehab or a treatment center?

No. We are not a rehab, a detox, a hospital or a medical provider, and we do not have medical staff. A man must complete a rehab program before coming here. If you are still using today, write to us anyway and we will help you find a treatment facility.

Who was Joseph?

Joseph Watts. His family called him Joey. He was a son, a brother, a grandson, a cousin, a nephew and a friend, a fisherman and an outdoorsman with a laugh that carried. The home is named for him and exists so that other families do not lose what his family lost.

Do you take women?

Not today. We house men eighteen and older. We have prayed for years about a home for women and it is not here yet. If you are a woman in need of help, please still write to us — we know the coast and we will point you somewhere real.

What denomination are you?

We are a Christian home rooted in local Southern Baptist congregations, and we work alongside Michael Memorial Baptist Church and the Grace Point Campus. Men are not required to share our convictions when they arrive. They are required to attend and to be respectful.

Applying

How do I apply?

Through the online application. It is long — give yourself an hour and use the Save for later button. See our walk-through: how to apply, step by step.

Start the application

Why is the application so long?

Because we are asking you to live in a house with other men who are fighting for their lives, and we need to know who is coming. Contacts, treatment history, medications, employment, probation and your testimony all tell us how to help you and how to keep the house safe.

Can I contact you instead of applying?

The fastest way to reach us is the application, because it puts everything in front of staff at once. If you need to talk to a person first, email jeffyork@thejosephhome.org and someone will get back to you.

Do I need to have finished rehab already?

Yes, or be about to. That is the one hard requirement. We cannot supervise a withdrawal — detox from alcohol and some medications is medically dangerous and needs a doctor.

Will a criminal record stop me?

Not automatically. Men here have records, probation officers and court dates. What ends an application is dishonesty, not history. Answer every question truthfully, including the sex offender question.

I am on probation. Is that a problem?

No, and the application has a section for it. Give us the dates and your officer’s information so we can work with, not around, the people supervising you.

I take prescribed medication. Can I come?

The application asks for your medications, dosages and prescriber so that staff know what is in the house and how to help. Bring your prescriptions and keep your doctor. For families

How do I get my son or husband in?

He has to apply himself. That is not bureaucracy — a man who will not fill out the form is not ready to do the year. You can sit beside him while he does it.

Can I speak to staff about him?

Yes, and we want to hear from you — you know him better than we do. What staff can share back with you is limited by his own consent, which the application asks about.

He wants to leave. What do I say?

I love you. I am not coming to get you. Talk to the staff tonight. The third-week phone call is the most predictable event in this house. See loving him without rescuing him.

Is there anything for the family?

Yes. Our Hope family ministry takes a whole-family approach with weekend activities for the families of our men. Celebrate Recovery also has meetings for families, and Al-Anon and Nar-Anon meet on the coast.
