02 · Treatment first

If you need treatment first.

Joseph’s Home is a sober living home, not a rehab. We cannot supervise a withdrawal, and coming off some substances without medical supervision is dangerous. If you have not been through a programme yet, that is the step before this one — and these are the people who can find you a bed.

Come back when you are through it. Finishing a treatment programme is the first of our four steps, not a reason we turn people away. Men come here from Home of Grace and from programmes all over the coast.

  • FindTreatment.gov — the federal treatment locator. Search by ZIP for detox, residential and outpatient programmes, with what each one accepts for payment.
  • SAMHSA national helpline — 1-800-662-4357 — free, confidential, 24 hours a day, every day, in English and Spanish. They make referrals; they do not sell anything.
  • Mississippi Department of Mental Health helpline — 1-877-210-8513 — 24 hours a day. State-certified providers, including withdrawal management and residential treatment.
  • Home of Grace, Vancleave — (228) 826-5283 — Christ-centred, three months, men’s and women’s campuses, running since 1965. 14200 Jericho Road, Vancleave, MS 39565.
  • 988 — call or text, any hour, for a crisis that is about staying alive tonight rather than finding a bed next week.
Search FindTreatment.gov Home of Grace

A word of warning. Search “rehab near me” and most of the top results are advertising, not directories — the number connects to whoever paid for the listing. The four above are a government locator, two public helplines, and one programme we know directly.

Every week, all over the coast

Church and Celebrate Recovery

Church is not optional here, and neither is Celebrate Recovery. This is where the fight gets named out loud, in front of people who are fighting the same one.

Every man in the house is in church, and in church activities, from his first week. We attend Celebrate Recovery at Michael Memorial and at the Grace Point Campus, and men are welcome at any of the meetings listed below.

The reason is simple. A meeting is the one hour in the week where a man says the true thing out loud in a room of people who will not flinch at it. You can white-knuckle a lot of things in private. You cannot white-knuckle honesty. Celebrate Recovery is Christ-centered, it is structured, and it does not let a man stay vague about what he is actually dealing with.

Therefore, confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person has great power as it is working.

James 5:16 · ESV

What to expect at a meeting

Most meetings open with a meal. That is not a formality — it is how a room full of strangers becomes a room full of people who know each other’s names. After the meal there is teaching or a testimony, and then the room splits into open share groups, separated by gender, where what is said stays in the room.

Nobody is going to make you talk on your first night. Come, eat, sit, listen. That is a full night’s work if it is your first one.

If you are not in the house yet: you do not have to be a resident of Joseph’s Home to walk into any of these meetings. They are open. Show up, eat, and see what you think.

Mental health matters

Celebrate Recovery is not only for addiction, and it does not pretend that faith replaces treatment. Men come through these rooms carrying grief, anxiety, anger, and the aftermath of things done to them as well as things they did. There are Mental Health Matters groups and a Welcome Home track for military and veterans in the wider Celebrate Recovery network.

If you need a doctor or a counselor, we will say so and help you find one. We are not a medical provider, and no meeting is a substitute for one.
