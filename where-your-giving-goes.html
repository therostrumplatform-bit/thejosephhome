Where your giving goes

You should know what your money does before you give it. Here is the plain accounting.

Joseph’s Home · Saucier, Mississippi · 2 min read · updated August 2026

“Each one must give as he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7 · ESV

What giving pays for

  • Beds. Rent, utilities, and the ordinary running of a household of men.
  • Teaching. Bibles, study materials, and the people who do the discipling.
  • Education. GED and college preparation for men who need it, and the community learning programmes we take part in.
  • Health and transport. Getting men to appointments, to work, and to church.
  • The gap. The week a man arrives with nothing and needs boots for a job that starts Monday.

The honest case for a small home

We are not a national charity. There is no marketing department here, and no one at this house is getting rich. The distance between your gift and a man’s bed is about as short as it gets — and you can drive over, knock on the door and see what you paid for, which is not true of most places asking for your money.

The corresponding honesty: small means fragile. A handful of monthly givers is worth far more to a house like this than a large one-off gift, because it lets us commit to a man for twelve months knowing the bed will still be there in month nine.

Ways to give

  • Monthly, at any amount. This is the most useful thing you can do.
  • One-off, any time, through the Give button on this site.
  • In kind — household goods, work clothes, tools, a vehicle. Call first so we ask for what is actually needed.
  • The golf tournament and community events, which are as much about the men meeting the community as about the money.
  • Your time, which we will always take.

Questions before you give? Call jeffyork@thejosephhome.org and ask anything you like about how the home is funded and run. If a charity cannot answer that plainly, do not give to it — including us.

Questions this did not answer? Email jeffyork@thejosephhome.org, or read the frequently asked questions.
