For families

Loving him without rescuing him

Every family reaches the point of wondering whether help has become the problem. It is a fair question and it deserves a straight answer.

Joseph’s Home · Saucier, Mississippi · 2 min read · updated August 2026

“For each will have to bear his own load.”

Galatians 6:5 · ESV

Two verses sit five lines apart in Galatians 6. Bear one another’s burdens. Every man shall bear his own burden. They are not a contradiction. The first is a crushing weight nobody carries alone. The second is the pack a grown man carries himself. Almost every family in this situation has, out of love, taken hold of the second one.

What rescue looks like

  • Paying a debt he made, so that he never meets it
  • Calling his employer with the excuse he gave you
  • Arguing with the house about a rule he agreed to
  • Driving four hours at midnight because he was persuasive
  • Keeping his secret from the rest of the family

Each of these is love. Each of them also removes the consequence that was doing the teaching.

What support looks like

  • Showing up when he is doing the hard thing, not when he is avoiding it
  • Saying “I love you and no” in the same breath, and meaning both
  • Telling him the truth about what he did, once, without a speech
  • Praying for him out loud, where he can hear it
  • Being consistent for a year, which is more convincing than being intense for a week

Sentences that help

I am not going to do that, and I am not going anywhere.

That sounds like something to take to the staff tonight.

I believe you can do this. I do not believe I should do it for you.

I forgive you. I still need time.

The fear underneath

Every parent asks the same question: what if I say no and something happens to him? Say it plainly to God, because he can take it, and then remember that you have already tried carrying it and it did not save him either. You are not his saviour. There is one, and he is better at it.

An exception worth naming. Boundaries are not the same as abandonment, and a medical emergency is not a boundary question. If he is in danger, call 911. If you need someone to talk to at any hour, call or text 988.
