Inside the home

What happens when a man falls

It is the question every man wants answered before he arrives and is too proud to ask. So here it is.

Joseph’s Home · Saucier, Mississippi · 2 min read · updated August 2026

“Whoever conceals his transgressions will not prosper, but he who confesses and forsakes them will obtain mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13 · ESV

Relapse is not a foreign event in this world. It is common, it is dangerous, and it is not the end of a man’s story unless he decides it is.

What we ask of you

Tell somebody, immediately, before you are found out. The distance between those two things — confessing and being discovered — is the distance between mercy and a long, slow collapse. Proverbs is blunt about it and so are we.

There will be a consequence. There is always a consequence, and hiding it makes it larger. What there will not be is contempt, and what there will not be is the end of your welcome.

A serious word about the risk

After a period of sobriety, your tolerance is gone. The amount that was ordinary for you a year ago can kill you now. This is a leading cause of overdose deaths after treatment, and men who relapse in secret die of it. If you have used and you are afraid, that fear is correct and it is the reason to speak, not to hide.

If somebody is unresponsive, call 911. Mississippi’s medical amnesty law offers certain protections to people who seek emergency help in an overdose. Call regardless.

What the house does

Every situation is handled by staff, individually, with the man in the room. What happens will depend on honesty, safety, whether you came forward, and what you need — which sometimes is a return to treatment rather than another week here. That is not a punishment. A house cannot provide medical care, and pretending otherwise is how people get hurt.

For the man reading this after a fall

You are not disqualified from grace. Not by this, not by the number of times, not by how recently you promised otherwise. The man who wrote most of the New Testament held coats at a stoning. Peter denied Christ to a servant girl three times in one night and was given the church.

Pick up the phone. That is the whole next step. Not a plan, not an apology tour, not a sufficient amount of shame — a phone call, today, to somebody in this house.

Right now, if it is bad: 911 for an emergency. Call or text 988 for free, confidential support at any hour. SAMHSA’s treatment referral helpline is 1-800-662-4357.
