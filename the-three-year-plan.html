Inside the home

Why three years

Ninety days was never going to do it. Here is what three years is for.

Joseph’s Home · Saucier, Mississippi · 2 min read · updated August 2026

“And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up.”

Galatians 6:9 · ESV

Joseph’s Home offers a three-year discipleship plan: twelve months living in the house, then two years of self-directed mentorship. Men ask why so long. The answer is arithmetic. Most men arrive having spent ten or fifteen years building the thing they are trying to leave. Thirty days interrupts it. Three years replaces it.

Months one to twelve: the house

You live here. You work. You go to church. You are discipled, which means somebody older in the faith takes responsibility for teaching you and is allowed to ask you anything. You are around men in the same fight, which does more work than any single conversation with any single staff member ever will.

What is really being built in this year is unglamorous: a sleep schedule, a work record, a church you belong to, a handful of men who have your number, and a hundred small instances of having said a true thing when a lie would have been easier.

Years two and three: mentorship

You leave the house. You do not leave the men. Self-directed mentorship means the relationship continues on your initiative — you keep meeting, you keep answering the phone, you keep being known by people who knew you at your worst and stayed.

This is the part most programs do not have, and it is the part that decides things. Year one gets a man sober and steady inside a structure. Years two and three are where he finds out whether it holds when the structure is gone. It does hold, but not by accident and not alone.

What we ask you to plan for

  • A job you can keep, not the fastest money available.
  • A church you actually join rather than attend. A man on his own on a Sunday is a man drifting.
  • Restitution where you can make it — debts, relationships, the apologies you have been putting off. Slowly and without demanding anything back.
  • Somebody younger in it than you. Around year two, you become useful to a man who is where you were. That is not a reward for finishing. It is part of the medicine.

If three years sounds impossible, do not sign up for three years. Sign up for the day in front of you and let the rest be God’s business. That is how every man who has finished it did it.
