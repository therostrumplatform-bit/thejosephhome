Inside the home

Your first thirty days

Nobody tells men what the first month feels like, so they assume the discomfort means it isn’t working. Usually it means it is.

Joseph’s Home · Saucier, Mississippi · 2 min read · updated August 2026

“For the righteous falls seven times and rises again.”

Proverbs 24:16 · ESV

Week one: the ground under you

You will be given a bed, a schedule, and a set of expectations you will not remember all of. You will meet men who arrived before you and who will tell you, without being asked, exactly how this works. Listen to them. Every house has an unwritten manual and it lives in the men who are already in it.

Sleep will be strange. It is strange for everyone. Your body is learning a rhythm it has not kept in years.

Week two: the schedule stops being an insult

Most men arrive with a private conviction that they should be exempt from something — the early hours, the chores, the meetings, the going to church twice. Around the second week that argument runs out of energy. What replaces it is a mild and unfamiliar sensation which turns out to be having done what you said you would do, several days in a row.

Week three: the wall

This is the week men leave. The novelty is gone, the work is real, somebody has annoyed you, and the voice that got you here starts making an old and very reasonable-sounding case that you have proved your point and could manage fine on your own now.

Tell somebody. That is the whole instruction. Not a counselor in an office three weeks from now — the man across the table at breakfast. Nothing that gets said out loud in this house has the power that it had in your head.

Week four: work

By now you are looking for a job or you are in one. We partner with local employers, and work here is not a way to pay us back — it is part of the discipleship. A man who works reestablishes something in himself that no amount of sitting in a circle will produce.

What is expected of you the whole time

  • Be where you said you would be.
  • Tell the truth the first time, including when it costs you.
  • Do your part of the house without being asked twice.
  • Go to church, and go to Celebrate Recovery.
  • When you want to leave, say so out loud before you pack.

For families reading this: the third-week phone call, the one where he sounds miserable and reasonable and wants you to come get him, is the most predictable event in this house. It is not a sign that something has gone wrong. See loving him without rescuing him.
