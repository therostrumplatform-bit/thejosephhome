Before you apply

What to write when we ask for your testimony

Men freeze at these two boxes. They think testimony means a polished church story with a tidy ending. It does not.

Joseph’s Home · Saucier, Mississippi · 2 min read · updated August 2026

“One thing I do know, that though I was blind, now I see.”

John 9:25 · ESV

A testimony is simply your account of what happened and where God has been in it. The man in John 9 gave one of the shortest in scripture and it has outlived every clever argument made against him. He did not have theology. He had before, and after, and the truth about which was which.

What we are reading for

  • Honesty about the bottom. Not gory detail. Just the truth about what it cost you and who it cost.
  • Ownership. Whether the story is something that happened to you or something you did. Both are usually true. We want to hear you say the second part.
  • What changed, or what you want to change. If nothing has changed yet, say that. “I am not sure I believe any of this yet, but I know I cannot keep living the way I have been” is an honest sentence and we have accepted men who wrote it.
  • Whether you want discipleship or a bed. Both are understandable. Only one of them is what this house is.

What we are not reading for

Spelling. Grammar. Bible knowledge. Length. Whether you can quote scripture, whether you were raised in church, whether you sound like a preacher. Write it the way you would say it to a man across a table.

If you are stuck, answer these

  1. When did you first know it had a hold on you?
  2. What is the thing you regret most? Name it.
  3. Who has been hurt, and what have you done about it?
  4. What have you already tried, and why do you think it did not hold?
  5. What do you want your life to look like in three years?
  6. Why here, and why now?

Six paragraphs. That is a testimony.

Why should we choose you?

This question makes men defensive, so hear the intent. There are more men who need a bed than there are beds. We are not asking you to sell yourself or to promise you will be the best man who ever came through. We are asking what you are prepared to do, and what you will bring to the house — whether you will work, whether you will show up, whether you will be a brother to the other men or a problem for them.

One caution. Do not write what you think we want to hear. Staff here have read thousands of these and have been lied to by better liars than either of us. The honest application is also the easier one to write.
